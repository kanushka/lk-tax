How to Calculate Income Tax for Non-Resident Sri Lankan Citizens

Many Sri Lankan citizens, including those with dual citizenship, who are not considered
residents for income tax purposes (non-resident individuals), often inquire about the
computation of income tax on their earnings derived from Sri Lanka. The following
explanation is provided to clarify how their income tax liability is calculated.

1) Who qualifies as a non-resident individual for income tax purposes?

The Section 69(1) of the Inland Revenue Act, No. 24 of 2017 (as amended) (hereinafter referred
to as “IRA”) states the individuals who shall be a resident in Sri Lanka for a year of assessment.
They are the individuals who,

(a) resides in Sri Lanka;

(b) is present in Sri Lanka during the year and that presence falls within a period or periods
amounting in aggregate to one hundred and eighty three days or more in any twelve month
period that commences or ends during the year;

(c) is an employee or an official of the Government of Sri Lanka and his spouse is posted
abroad during the year; or

(d) is an individual who is employed on a Sri Lanka ship, within the meaning of the Merchant
Shipping Act, during the period the individual is so employed.

Accordingly, other than those individuals, all other individuals shall be considered as non-
resident individuals.

Example 1: Mrs. Roshani Wickremasingha is an accountant working in Australia. She has
been living there with her spouse and children in a rented apartment since 2020. Her children
are studying in Australia, and her earnings are used for family expenses, with any remaining
amount deposited in Australian bank accounts. Occasionally, she remits foreign earnings to her
parents in Sri Lanka. Mrs. Wickremasingha also owns a home in Sri Lanka and has fixed
deposits there, in addition to being a member of the School Old Girls Association.

Although she maintains a home, bank accounts, and association membership in Sri Lanka, her
primary ties (such as her regular home, family, social life, and economic interests) are in

Australia. Therefore, Mrs. Wickremasingha is considered a non-resident for income tax

purposes in Sri Lanka, given that her main family, social, and economic dependencies are in
Australia rather than in Sri Lanka.

Example 2:

Mr. Nimesh Karunarathna received a foreign employment opportunity with a South Korean
company. He obtained an employment visa for a two-year period and left Sri Lanka on June 5,
2024. As a result, he is considered as a non-resident individual for income tax purposes for the
year of assessment 2024/2025.

2) Income Chargeable to Tax

The assessable income of non-resident individuals includes income earned from employment,
business, investments, or other sources during the year, to the extent that the income arises in
or is derived from a source in Sri Lanka. F urthermore, if a non-resident individual receives any
final withholding payments during the year, they are required to fulfill their income tax liability
accordingly.

3) Personal Relief

As per subsection (2) and (3) of Section 52 of the IRA.

(2) In arriving at the taxable income of an individual who is resident in Sri Lanka for a year
of assessment under section 3, the aggregate reliefs referred to in the Fifth Schedule to this
Act shall be deducted.

(3) In arriving at the taxable income of an individual who is not resident in Sri Lanka for a
year of assessment but is a citizen of Sri Lanka under section 3, the relief referred to in
paragraph 2(a) of the Fifth Schedule to this Act shall be deducted.

Therefore, non-residents but citizen individuals are entitled for the deduction of personal relief
(Year of Assessment 2024/2025 = Rs. 1,200,000, Year of Assessment 2025/2026 and thereafter
Rs. 1,800,000). However, they are not entitled for other reliefs (relief on rental income from
an investment asset or relief for the acquisition of solar panels).

4) Final Withholding Payments

As per Section 88 (1A)(C) of the IRA, interest paid to or treated as being derived by a non-
resident individual who is a citizen of Sri Lanka:

Provided however, the following interest amounts shall not be deemed as final withholding
payments to such non- resident individual who is a citizen of Sri Lanka: -

(i) such amount of interest paid and falling within the relief threshold in paragraph
2(a) of the Fifth Schedule to this Act;
or

(ii) such amount calculated by deducting the total of other sources of assessable income
(total assessable income other than interest) from the relief threshold if the total of
assessable income from other sources does not exceed the relief threshold.

Accordingly, interest income earned by non-resident individuals who are citizens of Sri Lanka
is generally treated as a final withholding payment. However, since they are entitled to personal
relief, the portion of interest income that falls within the personal relief threshold is treated as
part of their assessable income, while any excess is treated as a final withholding payment.

If a non-resident Sri Lankan citizen has other sources of assessable income, the statute allows
the personal relief to be deducted from those sources first. In such cases, the interest income
(either in whole or in part, depending on the level of assessable income) will then be treated as
a final withholding payment.

** Any payment derived by a non-resident individual who is not a citizen of Sri Lanka is
considered a final withholding payment for income tax purposes.
